Worked example: a 40 ft by 30 ft roof at 6/12 pitch has a footprint of 1,200 sq ft. With a multiplier of 1.118 the roof area is about 1,342 sq ft. Add 10% waste and the area is about 1,476 sq ft, or 14.76 squares, which needs 45 bundles (14.76 × 3, rounded up).

How many shingles in a bundle?

A bundle of standard 3-tab shingles typically covers about 33.3 square feet, which is one third of a roofing square. The exact number of shingles in a bundle varies by brand and product, and a typical 3-tab bundle contains about 29 shingles. Architectural shingles are thicker, so a bundle of those contains fewer shingles but covers the same area per bundle in most cases. Always check the packaging for the product you plan to buy, because that is the only reliable figure for that product.

How many bundles of shingles in a square?

There are typically 3 bundles of standard 3-tab shingles in a square. A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface, so 3 bundles at about 33.3 square feet each make one square. This is the figure used by the calculator above. For architectural shingles, the bundles-per-square figure can differ, so confirm it from the maker's data sheet before you order.

FAQs

How many shingles in a bundle?

A bundle of standard 3-tab shingles typically covers about one third of a roofing square, which is about 33.3 square feet. That is why three bundles are usually counted as one square. The number of individual shingles in a bundle varies by product, so check the packaging for the exact product you are buying.

How many bundles of shingles in a square?

There are typically 3 bundles of standard 3-tab shingles in a square. One roofing square is 100 square feet. Architectural shingles are thicker and heavier, so the number of bundles per square can vary — check the maker's packaging before ordering.

How many bundles do I need for my roof?

Measure the roof area in square feet, divide by 100 to get squares, add waste, then multiply by 3 bundles per square for standard 3-tab shingles. Use the calculator above and round up to whole bundles, because suppliers do not sell part of a bundle.
