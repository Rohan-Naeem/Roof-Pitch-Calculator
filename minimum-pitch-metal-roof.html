Minimum Pitch for Metal Roof

This guide explains the minimum pitch for a metal roof and the minimum slope for a metal roof, why the minimum exists, and what to do on a low-slope roof. Minimums vary by product, so always confirm the figure with the manufacturer and with local building code.

Minimum pitch for a metal roof

As a general guide, most standing-seam metal roofs need a minimum pitch of 3:12. That means the roof rises at least 3 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run, an angle of about 14.0 degrees. Some standing-seam systems are rated for 2:12, and a few specialist low-slope systems go lower still, but only with details specified by the maker, such as sealed seams and a full membrane underlayment. Do not assume a low pitch is fine because a panel looks watertight — the rating is in the maker's instructions, not in the appearance of the panel.

Minimum slope for a metal roof

The minimum slope for a metal roof depends on the profile. Standing-seam panels are generally the most forgiving at low slopes. Corrugated, ribbed and exposed-fastener panels usually need a steeper pitch, because water can work its way under laps and around fasteners on a shallow slope. If your roof is below 3:12, ask the maker for the rated minimum for that exact profile before you buy.

Why a minimum pitch matters

On a shallow roof, rain and melting snow move slowly and can sit against seams, laps and fasteners. Wind can also drive water uphill under a panel. A steeper pitch lets water run off before it can find a gap. That is why low-slope roofs need more careful flashing, sealed seams, and a membrane underlayment that keeps working even if a little water gets under the metal.

Low-slope roofs and snow

Metal sheds snow well once it starts moving, but on a low pitch snow can sit and add load. In snow areas, the pitch, the snow load in the local code, and the strength of the roof structure all need to be checked together. A higher pitch is often the simpler answer in heavy-snow areas, but the final choice should follow the local code and a professional's advice.

Check before you build: minimum pitch varies by product and by code. Confirm the rated minimum with the manufacturer for the exact panel system, and confirm the local building code for your area.

FAQs

What is the minimum pitch for a metal roof?

As a general guide, most standing-seam metal roofs need a minimum pitch of 3:12. Some systems are made for lower pitches, down to 2:12 or even 1/4:12, but only with special details, sealant and underlayment specified by the maker. The exact minimum varies by product, so check the manufacturer's instructions for the system you plan to use.

What is the minimum slope for a metal roof?

The minimum slope for a metal roof depends on the profile. Standing-seam panels are generally rated from 3:12, while corrugated and ribbed panels often need a steeper pitch. Low-slope metal roofs need careful flashing, sealed seams and the right underlayment to stop water backing up under the panels.

Can a metal roof go on a low-slope roof?

A metal roof can go on a low-slope roof if the system is designed for it and the maker's minimum pitch is met. Below that minimum, water can sit on the roof and work its way under seams. In that case a membrane or low-slope roofing system is usually the safer choice.
