Lean-to roof pitch

A lean-to roof is a single-slope roof that rests on a taller wall at one side and a lower wall at the other. Its pitch is worked out the same way as any other roof: measure the rise from the low side to the high side, measure the horizontal run across the span, and divide the rise by the run. Because lean-to roofs are often small, a pitch of 2/12 to 4/12 is common, but the roofing material sets the real minimum — check the maker's guidance for the product you plan to use.

Typical shed roof pitches

Most garden sheds use a pitch between 3/12 and 6/12. A 3/12 pitch is simple to build and keeps the shed low, while a 6/12 pitch sheds rain and snow better and gives more headroom inside. In snow areas, choose the higher end of that range where the structure allows it.

FAQs

What is a good pitch for a shed roof?

A good pitch for a shed roof is usually between 3/12 and 6/12, depending on the size of the shed, the roofing material and the local climate. A lower pitch is simpler to build, while a higher pitch sheds rain and snow better. Check the roofing maker's minimum pitch before you choose.

What is a lean-to roof pitch?

A lean-to roof pitch is the pitch of a single-slope roof that leans against a taller wall or building. It is worked out the same way as any other pitch — rise over run — and it is usually a low pitch, often 2/12 to 4/12, because a lean-to roof is often a small addition.
